How to Calculate EMI: Formula and Worked Example

An EMI is the fixed amount you pay every month to repay a loan. Here is the formula, one example worked out in rupees, and what each payment is made of.

The EMI formula

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ [(1 + r)n − 1]

  • P is the loan amount (the principal).
  • r is the monthly interest rate. Divide the yearly rate by 12, then by 100. A 9% yearly rate gives r = 0.0075.
  • n is the number of monthly payments. A 5-year loan has n = 60.

Worked example

Take a loan of ₹10,00,000 at 9% a year for 5 years.

  1. P = 10,00,000
  2. r = 9 ÷ 12 ÷ 100 = 0.0075
  3. n = 5 × 12 = 60
  4. (1 + r)n = (1.0075)60 ≈ 1.5657
  5. EMI = 10,00,000 × 0.0075 × 1.5657 ÷ (1.5657 − 1) ≈ ₹20,758

What each payment is made of

Every EMI has two parts: interest on the balance you still owe, and repayment of the principal. Interest is highest in the first month and shrinks as the balance falls. For the example above, the first payment splits like this:

  • Interest for month 1: 10,00,000 × 0.0075 = ₹7,500
  • Principal repaid: 20,758 − 7,500 ≈ ₹13,258
  • Balance after month 1: about ₹9,86,742

Over the months the interest share falls and the principal share rises, while the EMI stays the same.

How rate and tenure change your EMI

  • A longer tenure lowers the monthly EMI but increases the total interest you pay.
  • A shorter tenure raises the EMI but reduces the total interest.
  • A lower rate reduces both the EMI and the total interest.
  • A larger down payment lowers P, which lowers everything else.

Frequently asked questions

What does EMI stand for?

EMI stands for equated monthly instalment: the fixed amount paid each month until the loan is cleared.

Is the EMI the same every month?

On a fixed-rate loan, yes. The split between interest and principal changes each month, but the total stays the same. On a floating-rate loan, the EMI or the tenure can change when the rate changes.

Why is so much of my first EMI interest?

Interest is charged on the outstanding balance, which is largest at the start. As you repay principal, the interest part of each EMI shrinks.

Does a prepayment reduce my EMI?

It depends on your lender. A part-payment usually either lowers the EMI or shortens the tenure. Ask your bank which option applies and whether any charges apply.
